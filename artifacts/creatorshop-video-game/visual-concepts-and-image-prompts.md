# CREATORSHOP: THE VIDEO GAME

Visual concepts and image-generation assets for a fashionable pixel-art social/life simulator where creativity has purchasing power.

## North Star

Make a modern spiritual successor to compact mid-2000s mobile social/life sims, but with Creatorshop's SaaS design language expanded into a walkable city. The result should feel like a product interface became an economy, a neighborhood, and a lifestyle fantasy.

Primary tagline: **Rewarding humans who make great things.**

Do not copy Miami Nights characters, maps, UI, sprites, logos, or layouts. Use it only as structural inspiration: readable mobile-scale pixel characters, compact city spaces, social progression, apartments, shops, nightlife, dating, stats, and fashion customization.

## Creatorshop Visual DNA

Use the existing Creatorshop brand system as the source of truth:

- Primary lime: `#A3FF38`, used as energy, approval, interaction, campaign availability, badges, branded signage, and occasional full-surface moments.
- Lime border: `#82F200`, used as structural edge around lime panels/buttons.
- Ink: `#171717`, used for primary controls, high contrast system surfaces, and main linework.
- Paper: `#FFFFFF`, used for main panels and clean transactional surfaces.
- Soft surface: `#FAFAFA`, used for cards, empty states, secondary panels, and calm UI.
- Line: `#E5E5E5`, used for borders, dividers, and grid structure.
- Muted text: `#737373`, used for metadata and supporting copy.
- Danger: `#DC2626`, reserved for destructive/error states.

Practical ratio for UI-heavy scenes: about 70 percent paper/soft surface, 20 percent ink/line work, 10 percent lime. City scenes may use broad, colorful environmental palettes, but lime should be reserved for Creatorshop infrastructure and important interactive moments.

The lime rule matters: lime is a surface/action accent, not paragraph text.

## Pixel Translation Rules

- Use crisp pixel art with readable forms at mobile-game scale.
- Use black/ink outlines where helpful, but keep UI panels clean and SaaS-like.
- Treat Creatorshop cards as premium digital objects, not fantasy RPG inventory.
- Translate rounded SaaS cards into pixel panels with lightly rounded corners, 1-2 px borders, and strong white/soft gray surfaces.
- Translate primary actions into dark ink buttons with white text, subtle inset highlight, and pressed-game affordance.
- Translate accent actions into lime panels/buttons with lime-border edges and black text.
- Translate the Creatorshop node symbol into city signage, campaign markers, subway/map nodes, premium collectibles, and small interaction beacons.
- Use the metal symbol only for premium, cinematic, or launch moments, not tiny dense UI.
- Use Satoshi-like clean grotesque typography for readable mockup text. For pixel-only assets, approximate with a modern square pixel font.

## Game Concept Pillars

1. **Cash or Content**
   Most purchases can be paid with money or with a creative action. Cash is immediate and private. Content is social, conditional, and can be more valuable.

2. **A Life Built Through Shops**
   The player rents, decorates, dresses, socializes, works, dates, posts, and grows through access to products, Packs, events, and brand relationships.

3. **Creatorshop As Urban Infrastructure**
   Creatorshop is not just a menu. It appears as storefront stickers, branded kiosks, product claim terminals, HQ signage, receipt panels, and map markers.

4. **Aspirational, Not Dystopian**
   The city is lively, youthful, fashionable, and slightly chaotic. Avoid generic cyberpunk, grim futurism, and monochrome green worlds.

## Core Art Directions

### Direction A: Product City

A bright compact city where shop windows look like Creatorshop cards. Streets have clean product-board signage, lime interaction beacons, and neutral/ink UI overlays. Best for gameplay screenshots and world exploration.

### Direction B: Interface Diorama

Pixel world scene with floating SaaS panels: product offer cards, Creator Pack cards, map tabs, notifications, and campaign status badges. Best for pitch deck visuals and feature explanation.

### Direction C: Fashionable Starter Life

Warmer domestic and social scenes: starter apartment, thrifted outfits, laptop/phone creation loop, mirror/selfie moments, and small wins. Lime appears in charger cable lights, app screens, badges, and posters.

### Direction D: Premium Campaign Moment

High-energy reward/completion screens with ink background, chrome node symbol, lime burst surfaces, Pack cards, and celebratory pixel particles. Best for POSTED, level-up, and unlock visuals.
